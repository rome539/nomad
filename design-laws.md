# The building laws

The few principles everything in this game is built against. They are not
aspirations — each one names a thing that actually shipped, and the example
is the proof. When a new feature argues with one of these, the feature is
wrong, not the law.

## 1. Couple to what exists — the watchman law

A new thing should change the VALUE of something that already exists before
it adds anything of its own. The best features make the old world deeper,
not bigger.

The bell door adds no key, no mob, no clock. It borrows the bell, and by
borrowing it, it makes the last watchman — a mob players killed freely for
loot — the one thing in the world worth keeping alive. Kill him and the
hours fall silent, and the door with them. The choice stays free; its weight
changed.

Ask of any new system: *what already exists that this makes worth more?*
If the answer is "nothing," the feature is parallel content, not depth.

## 2. Bias, never trigger

Systems may LEAN on each other — settled rain charges the spate, mass death
charges the wake, the watchman's death silences the bell's hours. They may
never FORCE each other: nothing is ever required to fire so that something
else can. A consequence is a weight on a choice, never a script.
(events-scope.md's law, stated once: "events may bias each other, never
trigger each other.")

## 3. A window, never a wall

A shut door costs you a come-back-later. It never seals you in: the way back
is always unkeyed, and a door that shuts behind a window leaves everyone
inside free to leave. The deep door's law, obeyed by all four secret doors.

## 4. Legibility

If a player can't see it, it doesn't ship. Every state names itself in the
room — the door says whether it is shut, trembling, buried, or open, and
says WHY. Refusals teach the rule instead of shrugging. The deep mark's post
reads the water where you work it; the bell's warning note is the cue to
run. Nothing is a mystery about how to open a thing; the world tells you,
and the telling is the tutorial.

## 5. Bite, not block

The cost of a miss is time, a detour, or redoing the work — never a dead
end. The tide takes the digging back, not the shovel. A wrong riddle answer
is answered, never punished.

## 6. Knowledge is loot

What you learn IS the reward. Doors teach their own laws; instruments are
discovered, not documented. A rare find that gains a purpose (the drowned
bell, the tide tally) is worth more than a new item.

## 7. Witnessed only

Nothing performs to an empty room. Habits fire on a meeting, an hour, or a
presence — never a rolling timer playing to nobody (mob-weirdness.md's
doctrine). The world does not put on a show for an empty slope.

## 8. The text is the art

There is no art budget, and there never was: the prose is the renderer.
Every line earns its place, and repeated beats never read the same way
twice. Done right, not overdone — a feature is finished when it is legible
and restrained, not when it has one more rider.
