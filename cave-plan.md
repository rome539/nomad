# The cave under the wood: the plan

Planning only. Nothing here is built. Each line is marked **decided** or
**proposed** (put forward, not yet agreed). Keep this file current as the plan
changes; it is the one place the plan lives.

## Size and shape (decided)

About **200 rooms** in three depths:

| Depth | Rooms | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Upper cave | ~60 | Natural cave. Mouths are daylit, everything past them is dark. Ledges, shelves and chimneys. Cougar and hound ground. |
| Middle | ~85 | The water: an underground river that sinks under the wood, runs through, and rises at the Spring Mire. Side passages, pools, two bat roosts, the diggers' camps. |
| Bottom | ~55 | Not a cave any more: the old flat dressed stone, laid tight, cold air coming up through the joints. The sleeper's ground. |

- Its own region, `cave`, with its own map layer drawn under the wood.
- No weather: the slate skips it as it skips the Halls and the Deep, and fog does not get in.
- No gates, no wake points, no dens inside it (the spawn rule).
- Dark rooms follow the same plate flow as every plate: day base, then night, then night with a torch. See Light below.

## Entrances (decided)

Five, each at least 14 steps from every other. Steps are to the nearest gate.

| Entrance | Steps | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Under-Roots | 15 | The one near a gate (the Timber Stack). Already the dark ground under the Sunken Wood. "Near" means about 15 steps; 4 is too close. |
| The Ridge cave | 35 | The one bear-cave entrance: the back of the cave under the ridge goes on down. A bear holding it guards that way. |
| The Spring Mire | 21 | The cave's river rises here; the way in is a wet crawl up the spring's throat. |
| The Buckled Ground (Old Growth) | 25 | The roots heaved the floor into waves; one heave broke through. |
| The Ferny Bank | 23 | Its text already hears water running somewhere under it. |

Five entrances because the builder's throat rule wants one independent route per
40 rooms behind the cut.

## Fighters (decided, "for now")

| Depth | Fighter | How it fights |
|---|---|---|
| Upper | **Cave cougar** (8-10, alone, hold land) | The region's animal, not its boss. Ambusher and climber: drops from a ledge, hits hard first, breaks off if the fight turns. Not a copy of the mountain cave lion. |
| Upper | **Blind hounds** (packs of 3-4) | No eyes, hunt by sound. A torch means nothing to them; moving and fighting draw them. |
| Middle | **The river-gulper** | Huge pale fish in the deep pools. Takes you from the edge: grabs and pulls toward the water. A different fight in the water than on the bank. |
| Middle | **Cave spider, grown big** | Lives in the roosts. A bite holds you; the longer held, the worse it goes. |
| Middle | **Bat swarm** | Many small hits, and it can put your torch out. |
| Bottom | **The diggers** | People. They call each other, go for your light and your pack, and run when it goes badly. |
| Bottom | **The sleeper's brood** | Smaller versions of the sleeper's body, guarding the way to it. |
| Bottom | **The stone-sleeper** (boss, working name) | Lies on the dressed stone. A crawler kind of thing: pale, four-legged, salamander-like, blind, toothed, wrinkled hide. Its sheet is ORIGINAL, written in words; never use the pale-crawler sheet as a reference, and never call it "boneless" (that draws a slug). |

Small life the hunters feed on: **cave rats** (eat the dead) and **pale
salamanders** (the pools). Bats fly out to the wood at dusk to feed and come back
by dawn. Every template needs a feeding route or it starves.

## Cougars and the wood (decided)

- Cougars come up into the wood to hunt (roe, young boar) and **drag their kills
  down into the cave**. That feeds the rats and leaves bones and drag trails as
  signs.
- Proposed: bears take a cougar's kill; a wolf pack runs a cougar off its
  ground; a lone wolf, the hush and the coyotes give way to it.

## The story (decided 2026-11-20)

It follows the lore canon (decided 2026-11-05): **the sickness came up from
below.** Under every fever grave are older bones, and under those the same flat
dressed stone laid tight with cold air coming up through its joints. The bottom
of this cave is that stone. **The cave is the way down to what the fever came
up out of**, the one place a player can walk the world's story to its start.

- **Upper:** wild, natural. Only the air is wrong: it always moves, always up,
  always cold.
- **Middle:** the first marks of people. Pick-work widening the squeezes, old
  props and ladders, a camp gone cold. When the fever came, the holding's people
  followed the cold air down to wall it up. They stopped mid-stroke like everyone else in the
  country. The ones still down there are the diggers.
- **Bottom:** the dressed stone, built by someone older than the holding. The
  Old Growth's "planted ring of oaks round a square where something stood" sits
  over it: the older people marked the place.
- **The sleeper** lies on the stone. Nothing ever says what it is, or whether
  the sickness is it. Evidence only, never explained.

Story calls (decided 2026-11-20):
1. **The diggers were the holding's own people, sent down to seal it.** When the
   fever came they followed the cold air down to wall it up. Evidence in the
   rooms: stoppings half-built across passages, stone and mortar carried down
   and never laid, tallies of the work. Like every other work in the country it
   stopped mid-stroke. Fits the canon: the holding's trades all stopped mid-stroke,
   and the people went away in a hurry.
2. **The cave's dressed stone and the Deep's older stone under the fortress are
   the same stone, the same people.** Two ends of one thing, never joined by a
   path.
3. **Nothing ever says the sleeper is the sickness.** The rooms show only
   evidence: the stone, the cold air coming up through its joints, the thing
   lying on it, and the fever graves above with the same stone under them. The
   player draws the line. (Don't telegraph; and it leaves the canon's open
   questions open.)

## Light (decided 2026-11-20; the sources proposed)

- **The upper cave is completely dark.** Every room past the daylit mouths needs
  a carried light. This is the lamp-man's and the cougars' ground.
- **The middle and the bottom are lit by their own light**, with some dark rooms
  here and there.
  - Proposed source, middle: living light. Glow-worms hung over the river and
    foxfire (the cold glow of rotting wood and fungus) on the walls, thick where
    it is wet. Dark rooms are the dry stretches where it does not grow.
  - Proposed source, bottom: the dressed stone itself gives off a faint cold
    light, never explained (evidence only). Dark rooms are where the diggers'
    work or a fall of rock covers the stone.
- Text and picture always match: a lit room's text names its light, its plate
  shows it, and a torch carried into it adds the torch plate's own warm light
  (the one light rule). Dark rooms use the usual night and night-torch plates.

## The elite: the lamp-man (decided 2026-11-20)

The cave's mini-boss, on the tier of the cave lion and the great devil crab
(about 90 health, 6-10 damage, armour 2; a person in leather, not an animal).

- **Who he is:** he kept the diggers' lamps: filled them, trimmed them, carried
  them down to the camps. He still walks the upper cave, between the mouths and
  the first camp, with one lamp lit.
- **How he fights** (reacting to the fight, never on a timer):
  - He goes for YOUR light first: a carried torch or lantern can be knocked
    out or snuffed by his blows.
  - In the dark he has the first strike and you miss more; you can only see
    him while his lamp is unshuttered.
  - Hurt, he shutters his lamp and is gone into the dark, then comes back at
    you from another side. With his lamp out he cannot be followed by sight.
  - Light he cannot reach (a fire in the room) takes his advantage away.
- **Loot:** his lamp (an epic light piece) and a trophy worth a lot;
  the four legendaries stay as decided.
- Dropped: the Walled-In (a digger walled in behind the stopping), held as an
  idea only.

## Events (proposed 2026-11-20)

Each one comes out of the cave's own things (the air, the river, the bats, the
diggers, the stone), the way the scree fall is the mountain's and the boil is
the warrens'. None of them is explained in text; the cave shows it.

1. **The river rises.** Rain on the wood above, and hours later the underground
   river comes up. The middle's low rooms flood, the sumps close, the water
   rooms are deep water, and the river-gulper reaches rooms it cannot reach
   dry. Cause and effect across regions, as the halls fog comes from the open
   ground's fog.
2. **The bats go out.** Every dusk the swarm pours up through the upper cave and
   out of the mouths, and every dawn it comes back. A moving hazard you stand
   aside from (the boil's shape): many small hits, carried flames knocked out.
   Daily, so the upper cave has a time you do not want to be in a passage.
3. **The roof gives.** In the upper cave a stretch of roof comes down: a sound
   that carries, a bruise if you are under it, the dust putting flames out, and
   the passage choked for a while (a window, never a wall; another way round is
   always open).
4. **The glow goes out.** The middle's living light dies back for a spell (the
   glow-worms and foxfire dark together) and the lit depth is as black as the
   upper cave: ambush weather for the spider and the hounds.
5. **The work.** The sound of picks from below: the diggers take up the work
   again and every digger leaves his camp for the stopping. The camps stand
   unguarded (their boxes in reach), and the way to the bottom is full of men.
6. **The breath.** The Deep's exhale already rolls a cold current through every
   below-ground room and kills open flames. The cave's stone is the Deep's stone,
   so the cave breathes with it: the same beat reaches here, the breathing stone
   cannot be read while it lasts, and the dark is complete.
7. **The stone stirs** (rare). The cold air surges up everywhere at once: out of
   every mouth into the wood, and up through the joints under every fever grave
   in the world, where a close look finds frost on the graves that day. The
   sleeper's brood come up out of the bottom into the middle until it settles.
   Evidence only; nothing says why.

## The secret door (decided 2026-11-20)

The game's secret doors so far each take a different kind of key: what you
KNOW (the riddle door), WHEN it is (the moon door), where the WATER is (the tide
door), what you HEAR (the bell hatch). They keep the deep door's law: a window,
never a wall; the way back is always unkeyed; never on a route already open; no
hint text, silence after a wrong try.

**The breathing stone. Its key is the AIR**, the one thing the whole
cave is about.
- At the bottom, a chamber floored with a ring of **nine** dressed stones, the
  same ring as the nine oaks of the Standing Oaks above in the Old Growth.
- Cold air comes up through the joints of only ONE of the nine, and **the
  stone keeps changing**: it turns over through the day, AND every wrong pry
  moves the air to another stone. Trying every stone does not work (the target
  moves each miss) and remembering does not work (it is never the same stone
  twice running). The only way in is to read the cave each time.
- **The cave tells you, never the game.** No hint lines, only what the room
  shows, and the tell varies so it is never one fixed word to look for: a flame
  leaning toward the stone, dust lifting off its joints, a thin whistle in one
  seam, frost on one stone's face while the rest are damp. Up top at the
  Standing Oaks a close look shows the same: one oak's ground cold, its leaves
  stirring with no wind.
- Pry the stone the cave points at and it tilts up on its edge, steps going down
  under it, the cold pouring up: a `down` exit for a few minutes, open to anyone
  in the room, then the stone settles. A wrong stone does not move and nothing
  is said, but the air has moved.
- **Behind it: the under-stone**, a small hidden part (about 6-8 rooms, NOT counted
  in the 200; extra on top of them) where the older people's own things lie: the box on the dressed
  stone (opened with the sleeper's key) moves here, with the best of the
  bottom's epics and the strongest evidence of the older people. The sleeper is
  NOT behind it; the boss stays reachable without the puzzle.

Alternative held: the diggers' stopping, a half-built wall taken down with a
digger's pick (a key more than a puzzle).

## Loot (decided 2026-11-20; about 45-50 new items)

Follows the gear rules in force: the forge makes the rares (each needs a cave
trophy worth 5+), the keeper sells nothing above uncommon, no piece is matched
or beaten by one of the same or lower rarity in its slot, legendaries are
lateral (a trait, not bigger numbers). Names, numbers and wording go to review
before anything is built.

1. **Gear, about 25-30 pieces, every slot.**
   - Upper: hunter's gear from what you kill (cougar-hide cloak, hound-leather
     boots, claw and fang blades). Commons, uncommons, forged rares.
   - Middle: the diggers' kit, CARRIED on the diggers (seen worn, taken by
     killing them): picks that fight (blunt), a hard hat, a leather apron,
     crawling pads, a belt lamp.
   - Bottom: epics in every slot, left on the dressed stone by whoever was down
     there before the diggers.
2. **Four legendaries (decided),
   each with its own signature ability.**
   - The sleeper's, on the stone when it dies.
   - The river-gulper's, in its gut: something it swallowed whole, a digger and
     what he carried.
   - The last foreman's: a rare cousin of the diggers who still gives orders.
   - The great cougar's: a rare bloodline wearing an old collar nobody can
     explain.
3. **Trophies** from every creature: cougar pelt and claws, a hound's eyeless
   skull, spider silk and fang, bat leather, gulper bone, brood hide, a digger's
   tally tag, and a boss trophy from the sleeper.
4. **Boxes:** locked strongboxes at the diggers' camps (the usual strongbox
   keys); the foreman's box at the deepest camp (his key); one box on the
   dressed stone (a key from the sleeper).
5. **Food and supplies:** salamander, bat, cave fish, pale fungus off the walls,
   the diggers' oil and candles.
6. **A rare cousin for every fighter**, carrying cigarettes and keys as every
   other cousin does.
7. Cave items get their own item-picture sheets (5x5 grids cut into icons).
